### Current Behavior

Hello, I searched for this bug and found sort-of mentions of this issue but not necessarily as simple as mine, as all of those seem to have compounding circumstances.

I am new to Headscale and don't understand why my Mom's laptop is suddenly being marked as EXPIRED for no good reason?

`| false     | 2026-06-06 11:28:17 | 1970-01-01 00:02:03 | offline   | yes`

It looks like this and I don't understand why.  The "yes" in the far right corner indicates it's the only expired node of 20, yet nothing different has changed on my end as far as adding that client is concerned?  

What is causing this?

How do I revert the status from expired to not expired without forcing annoying re-adding of the server?  This is very tedious and a terrible solution when dealing with non-tech savvy people

Any help would be appreciated, thank you.  The client in question is just a simple Windows 11 laptop.

### Expected Behavior

For the client to not spontaneous-expire when they shouldn't?

> How do I revert the status from expired to not expired without forcing annoying re-adding of the server? This is very tedious and a terrible solution when dealing with non-tech savvy people

headscale nodes expire -d -i $ID

With #3285 zero expirations were fixed to `NULL` but in this case the timestamp is slightly off. I've also found a node with this exact timestamp: `1970-01-01 00:02:03` that is not covered by the fix. Representation in the database: `1970-01-01 01:02:03+01:00`

nblock

Created: 2026-06-12T18:16:00Z; updated: 2026-06-12T18:16:00Z

> What is causing this?

This is caused by a logout via the tailscale CLI/GUI client. It [sets the expiration to `time.Unix(123, 0)`](https://github.com/tailscale/tailscale/blob/7d18a06292e81f73c8face9ae503b9c16914ea55/control/controlclient/direct.go#L800-L801).

This is set to `now` since Headscale 0.29.0-beta.3: https://github.com/juanfont/headscale/commit/c5f3d5c28d628ef675210d172915751016f2b504
