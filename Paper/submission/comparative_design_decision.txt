Comparative-design decision — 15 October 2026

Decision: do not collect comparative account-role outcomes at this stage.
Additional repositories would test the reach of the Headscale description, but
would not by themselves validate its proposed responsibility-routing mechanism
or establish a new contribution. Prioritize a falsification-oriented mechanism
test, with explicit competing explanations and contradictory cases. Human
responsibility/outcome coding remains deferred; this decision does not replace
it with automated labels or claim submission readiness.

This feasibility assessment was made after observing Headscale results and
before inspecting comparator account-role outcomes. It is a prospective design
decision for a possible extension, not preregistration of the original study.
Only official documentation, repository metadata and aggregate merged-PR search
counts were inspected. No comparator PR/review dataset was saved, and no role
rates, reviewer distributions or author/integrator overlaps were measured.

Candidate relevance and exclusions

NetBird (netbirdio/netbird) is the closest candidate: a self-hostable Go platform
with a WireGuard overlay and public management, signal and relay components.
Its repository also includes the client. Whole-repository results therefore
would not isolate a server-maintenance population comparable to Headscale.
Its root license identifies BSD-3-Clause parts and separately AGPLv3 server
directories; the API's NOASSERTION license label is not evidence of no license.
Primary sources, retrieved 15 October 2026:
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/netbirdio/netbird/main/README.md
https://docs.netbird.io/selfhosted/selfhosted-guide
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/netbirdio/netbird/main/LICENSE

Nebula (slackhq/nebula) is a defensible architectural contrast within self-hosted
Go overlay networking. Its PKI, peer-local firewall and lighthouse discovery
model differ from a centrally managed coordination server. It must not be
presented as an otherwise matched Headscale control. Official documentation:
https://nebula.defined.net/docs/ (retrieved 15 October 2026).

Netmaker (gravitl/netmaker) supplies an additional, closer self-hosted Go server
candidate. Its open-source server manages WireGuard networks; its Apache core
and separately licensed pro/ material require explicit artifact-scope handling.
It should not be omitted merely because a different candidate gives a more
interesting role pattern. Official repository and licensing sources:
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gravitl/netmaker/develop/README.md
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gravitl/netmaker/develop/LICENSE.md
(retrieved 15 October 2026).

Tailscale (tailscale/tailscale) is contextual upstream evidence rather than a
matched self-hosted coordination-server control. The public repository contains
clients and other components, while Tailscale's hosted coordination server is
proprietary. Its relationship with Headscale also prevents treating the pair as
independent ecosystem replications. Official sources:
https://github.com/tailscale/tailscale
https://tailscale.com/opensource
(retrieved 15 October 2026).

If an extension is justified later, fix the candidate inventory before outcomes:
public, non-fork, non-archived repositories with a Go core, an independently
deployable encrypted overlay/VPN function, and documented public PR workflows.
Record every inclusion, exclusion and architecture/scope distinction. Use the
same UTC merge window, 2025-10-01 inclusive to 2026-10-01 exclusive, with four
complete calendar quarters. Include eligible merges regardless of PR creation
date. Neither convenient sample sizes nor observed role differences should
determine project selection. This is a purposive domain comparison, not a
probability sample of open-source projects.

Feasibility counts and collection budget

Authenticated GitHub metadata/count requests on 15 October 2026 reported:
  netbirdio/netbird: 1,141 merged PRs in the proposed window;
  slackhq/nebula:      258;
  gravitl/netmaker:    373;
  tailscale/tailscale: 1,769, contextual only.
Each query used /search/issues with q="repo:OWNER/REPO is:pr is:merged
merged:2025-10-01..2026-09-30" and per_page=1. Only total_count and the
incomplete_results flag were retained in the assessment; all flags were false.
Counts include bot-authored and human-account PRs, are collection-time indexed
counts rather than independently verified complete frames, and must not be
compared directly with the 314 human-account merges already available for
Headscale in that window. Exact human eligibility remains unmeasured elsewhere.

With one page per endpoint, detail + submitted reviews + lifecycle timeline
would cost approximately 3N requests: 4,197 for NetBird plus Nebula, or 5,316
including Netmaker, before author exclusions and discovery/pagination overhead.
File-scope checks add approximately N; both PR-thread and inline-comment
channels add approximately 2N. Actual pages can exceed these planning figures.
A probability sample capped at 25 merged PRs per project-quarter would need at
most 100 PRs/project and approximately 300 baseline requests/project, plus a
complete frame. Record inclusion probabilities and use design-weighted category
proportions; sampled account HHI is nonlinear and cannot inherit an unbiasedness
claim from weighted counts. A sample does not establish census completeness.

GitHub Search caps each query at 1,000 returned results, so NetBird's annual
query cannot itself supply a complete frame. Partition by disjoint UTC merge
intervals until every partition fits; reconcile count/pagination coverage and
validate returned PR details. Alternatively paginate a complete PR list and
filter by merged_at. Personal-token REST requests ordinarily have a 5,000/hour
limit and search has a separate 30/minute authenticated limit; secondary limits
also apply. Official endpoint/rate documentation, checked 15 October 2026:
https://docs.github.com/en/rest/search/search
https://docs.github.com/en/rest/pulls/pulls
https://docs.github.com/en/rest/using-the-rest-api/rate-limits-for-the-rest-api

Existing Client, collect_endpoint and load_endpoint helpers support budgets,
resumption and checksums. The current collector entry points hard-code
juanfont/headscale and are frozen provenance sources: a future comparison needs
a new repository-parameterized adapter rather than editing those scripts.
Reuse role_observation and lifecycle reconstruction after checking machine-merge
handling. Preserve bot/queue integrators as a separate stratum; do not silently
change the denominator or count them as additional human participants.

Contribution gate before expansion

CHAOSS already defines Self-Merge Rates around author-merges without a review
process, including broader review evidence than submitted review records alone.
Thus finding reviewed self-merges does not refute the published CHAOSS definition.
https://www.chaoss.community/kb/metric-self-merge-rates/
(retrieved 15 October 2026).

The TSE study Pull Request Decisions Explained already examines the same-user
author/integrator relationship and context-dependent PR decisions. The 2026
Reviewsaur industry report already studies self-merges jointly with approval
presence across 261 repositories. Its methodology acknowledges incomplete
PR-thread author/timestamp evidence and historical-comparison limitations. It is
an industry report, not established peer-reviewed ground truth; its overlap
nevertheless prevents claiming that our joint-role counting is itself new.
Primary sources, retrieved 15 October 2026:
https://yuyue.github.io/res/paper/PRDecision-TSE2022.pdf
https://www.reviewsaur.com/research
https://www.reviewsaur.com/research/methodology

A narrowly useful extension could test transportability of an explicitly named
measurement: per project-quarter, how much does the observed account-participation
distribution change when review evidence broadens from submitted reviews to
attributed, time-bounded discussion channels, under fixed PR/file populations?
These would be measurement differences, not validated review quality or repair
outcomes. Missing identities, informal review, historical state/head uncertainty,
monorepository composition, automation and shared ecosystem relationships remain
limitations. Pooling heterogeneous projects cannot supply independent replications
or a causal governance comparison. Proceed only if comparison with the closest
prior methods identifies a consequential unresolved question and a falsifiable
claim; otherwise it would multiply known descriptive findings. No comparator
account-role outcome collection has been started.
