Literature overlap assessment — 15 October 2026

Purpose and scope
Assess prior work on Headscale itself and on the proposed maintenance-analysis
questions. This is a targeted scoping search, not a completed systematic review.
Public web discovery was followed by primary publisher/author/institutional
sources where accessible. Full Scopus, Web of Science and Google Scholar corpus
coverage has not been established. Search ranking and inaccessible material limit
negative findings. The companion literature_search_log.json records queries,
verified sources and unresolved leads.

Same project: verified peer-reviewed study
Kjorveziroski et al., Software: Practice and Experience, 2024,
DOI 10.1002/spe.3329. The publisher and institutional full text were inspected.
Headscale is evaluated alongside Netbird and ZeroTier for VPN throughput and
Kubernetes API behavior under adverse network conditions. This is a deployment
performance study; it does not supply the proposed longitudinal maintenance-role,
PR-episode and responsibility-boundary analysis.
Primary source: https://doi.org/10.1002/spe.3329

Same repository appears in a mining-tool thesis: full text checked
The institutional PDF became accessible through an ordinary public GET on

aggregate developer skills and technology trends (pp.2,43–56), with no dedicated

prior work. This resolves the access lead without establishing exhaustive novelty.

Closest direction: strong prior precedent
- Bus Factor In Practice, ICSE-SEIP 2022: reviews and meetings supplement commit
  data, with engineer validation. Its senior-reviewer example directly precedes
  our observation that author-only activity omits some maintenance participants.
  https://doi.org/10.1145/3510457.3513082
- Review-aware ownership, ICSE 2016: distinguishes authoring from reviewing and
  relates ownership to software quality. Our matched role comparisons apply an
  established distinction.
  https://doi.org/10.1145/2884781.2884852
- Issue misclassification, ICSE 2013: directly precedes our concern about treating
  bug-labelled records as verified defects.
  https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/its-not-a-bug-its-a-feature-how-misclassification-impacts-bug-prediction/
- CODEOWNERS and review dynamics: a large-scale author preprint already studies
  declared ownership and responsibility distribution. A single static owner
  file cannot establish new policy effects.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05551v2
- OSS-health assessment, 2026: already considers governance concentration and
  organisational context. Role separation is not a new health framework.
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-026-10846-y

Other leads and non-academic material
A claimed 2026 sovereign-cluster preprint appeared in discovery, but its primary
Zenodo/DataCite record was not retrieved; it is not treated as verified
peer-reviewed related work. The FOSDEM maintainer presentation is contextual
testimony. OSSInsight and Linux Foundation dashboards already expose repository
activity; dashboard existence does not validate a new empirical mechanism.
False positives using HeadScale for transformer attention scaling were excluded
because they do not concern juanfont/headscale or the Tailscale control server.

Closer role-overlap precedents checked on 15 October
Zhang et al.'s TSE article (online 2022; final issue 2023) explicitly studies
submitter/integrator identity in PR decisions. CHAOSS already qualifies
author-merging by review, and Reviewsaur's practitioner methodology jointly
operationalizes author-merging and absence of another account's approval.
Therefore the new account-overlap census applies existing distinctions. Adding
similar repository counts alone would not establish a novel contribution.
Primary sources:
https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2022.3165056
https://www.chaoss.community/kb/metric-self-merge-rates/
https://www.reviewsaur.com/research/methodology

Implication for the manuscript
Do not claim the first academic study of Headscale, the first separation of
authors and reviewers, or a new validated bus-factor measure. The current
quantitative audit is case evidence supporting a narrower explanatory question:
how an explicit component-responsibility decision leads to a treatment outcome
and changes the interpretation of repository maintenance indicators.
That mechanism remains unvalidated, and actual human coding is deferred.
Another project name or additional activity charts cannot establish novelty.

Bounded negative finding
The inspected results did not establish a peer-reviewed study centered on this
specific Headscale maintenance-process question. This is not proof that none
exists. Resolving the mining-tool thesis does not establish comprehensive
bibliographic coverage or justify an exhaustive novelty claim.
