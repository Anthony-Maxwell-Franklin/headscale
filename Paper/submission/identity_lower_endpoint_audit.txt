INTERNAL METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT: MISSING-REVIEW IDENTITY LOWER ENDPOINT
Date: 2026-10-15

Purpose and disposition
This internal audit checks the attainability of an already computed identity
scenario. It makes no novelty claim and does not change the frozen reported
bounds, analysis scripts, manuscript, or underlying identity assignments. The
numerical tightening below changes no role-order conclusion. Mechanism evidence
therefore takes priority over further refinement of these endpoints.

Model and assumptions
Fix the N merged PRs with at least one attributed eligible review. PR i has a
fixed known-reviewer set K_i of size k_i > 0 and m_i eligible unattributed
submitted records. Every PR supplies one unit of credit, divided equally among
its distinct eligible reviewer accounts. Missing records may be duplicate,
self, bot, known-account, or additional eligible-account observations. Accounts
may be shared globally, but the model does not require such sharing and permits
enough otherwise unknown eligible non-author accounts to assign a fresh account
to each record. Known account identities and PR membership remain fixed.

The claim is conditional on this unconstrained assignment model. Enforced user-id
links, known bot types, a finite admissible-account pool, or other identity
constraints could prevent the proposed completion. In the affected fixed subset,
all 177 eligible missing records have null account metadata and author_association
NONE in the cached export. That metadata does not establish actual human status,
distinctness, or the likelihood of the completion. Known-account aliases, deleted
and informal reviews, and person-level identity are outside the model.

Constructive lower-endpoint proof
Let w_a be each account's total fractional credit across the N fixed PRs.
Replace each missing record in turn by its own globally fresh eligible non-author
account, never reused by another record.

If its previous account remains represented on that PR, or the old record was
ineligible, the PR's reviewer count rises from k to k+1. Each existing reviewer
loses delta = 1/[k(k+1)] and the new account receives 1/(k+1). If S is the sum of
the current global weights of those k existing reviewers, then S >= 1. The change
in sum_a w_a^2 is delta*(1-2S) < 0.

If the previous eligible account instead disappears from that PR, its credit
c = 1/k moves to the fresh account without changing the denominator. The change
in squared credit is -2*c*(w_old-c) <= 0, since w_old >= c.

Thus every assignment can be transformed without increasing HHI into the
all-missing-distinct-human completion. This completion is feasible under the
model, so it attains the sharp lower endpoint:

  L_a = sum_{i: a in K_i} 1/(k_i+m_i)
  H_min = [sum_a L_a^2 + sum_i m_i/(k_i+m_i)^2] / N^2.

This is exactly the existing all_missing_distinct_human_scenario_hhi, not a new
scenario or an imputation. The simplex upper endpoint remains conservative; the
entire interval has not been proved sharp.

Independent enumeration check
An ephemeral exact-rational check considered 82 small configurations and 37,796
record-to-identity assignments; no counterexample was found. With Python random
seed 3, it drew 30 configurations for each N in {1,2,3}, using nonempty known sets
drawn from {A,B,C}, m_i in {0,1,2}, and retaining configurations with sum_i m_i <= 4.
For each configuration it enumerated all assignments to an ineligible state,
the three known accounts, or up to M fresh accounts. Fractions.Fraction arithmetic
confirmed that the all-distinct completion equalled the minimum. This check
supports the constructive proof; it is not an additional empirical study or an
exhaustive verification of arbitrary population sizes. No enumeration script or
new endpoint is incorporated into the frozen analysis pipeline.
